# Recording script: one take, about 2:30

Read the quoted lines aloud. Do the **Show** line as you speak. Keep going through small stumbles.

## Set up (before pressing Record)

Open these tabs in one Chrome window, in this order:

1. The cards: run `open -a "Google Chrome" docs/video/cards.html` from the repo
2. `https://readback-web-7cmb.onrender.com/shopper`
3. `https://readback-web-7cmb.onrender.com/` (reload it so "Try it yourself" is fresh)
4. `https://readback-web-7cmb.onrender.com/judge` (optional, only if time allows)

Allow pop-ups for the site. Hide the bookmarks bar. Close anything with another company's logo.

Record: **Cmd+Shift+5** → Record Entire Screen → Options → pick your microphone → Record.
Stop: the stop button in the menu bar.

**Show:** press **R** to restart the card. It types itself; the three captions follow.

## 2. Claude drafts the terms (tab 2, shopper)

**Show:** press **Read it back to me**. Point at the terms card. Do not press approve on this page.

> Maya says it once. Claude turns her words into exact terms: one bag a month, up to sixty-five dollars, until March. Code checks every term. Claude drafts; it never decides.

## 3. She approves on PayPal (tab 3, home → Try it yourself)

**Show:** press **Create my key and approve on PayPal**. In PayPal's window, pause on the fingerprint, then agree.

> She approves on PayPal's own page. PayPal shows her agent's key fingerprint, the same sixteen characters her agent shows. Those terms now live inside PayPal, and nobody can edit them. Not the store, and not us.

## 4. Her agent buys; PayPal refuses the second (same section)

**Show:** press **Sign and buy one bag**. Then press it again.

> Her agent signs one exact order, inside her terms, and the store charges it through PayPal. Ask twice, and PayPal itself refuses the second.

## 5. The store cheats (same section)

**Show:** press **Charge my wallet without my agent**. Scroll to **Your two verdicts**.

> Now the store cheats. It charges her wallet itself, inside every limit. Same shopper, same agreement.
>
> On the left, her agent's charge: signed by the key she approved. On the right, the store's: no signature. So Readback says refund the buyer, against the very merchant running it. That's why it can be believed.

## 6. A case with history, and Claude's brief (tab 3, top of the page)

**Show:** scroll to the top. Press **Open the case** on Charge A. Wait for the rebuild (about five seconds). Then press **Explain this case in plain English**.

> When she disputes a charge, Readback rebuilds it from PayPal's own records and re-checks it right here in the browser. Her agent asked for this exact order, inside her limits, and it shipped. Uphold the charge.
>
> Claude explains why in plain English, citing a PayPal record for every sentence. But Claude doesn't decide. Her agent's signature and fixed rules do.
