## 1. Irreversibility of on-chain payments, and what recourse exists (merchant refunds, escrow contracts, arbitration, issuer freezes)

### Takeaway
A plain on-chain stablecoin transfer (x402 `exact`, a direct ERC-20 send) is final: there is no chargeback, and a refund is just a new transfer the merchant chooses to send. Recourse exists only where it was built in *before* payment: escrow/auth-capture contracts (the audited Coinbase/Base Commerce Payments Protocol, and the x402 `auth-capture` spec v1.1 whose EVM binding uses that escrow, give the payer no forced refund after capture; Circle's Refund Protocol adds an arbiter but is an unaudited research release). Issuer freezes are a law-enforcement and court-order tool, not a buyer-dispute remedy.

### Cited Findings
**Irreversibility (primary statements)**
- PayPal's own crypto terms (last updated May 19, 2026): "Even when a transfer is unconfirmed and pending, the transfer is irreversible." Its Connecticut disclosure warns that losses from fraudulent or accidental transactions "may not be recoverable" and that virtual currency transactions are irreversible. — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms and Conditions, May 19, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- Circle Research (Apr 17, 2025) frames the gap directly: stablecoin payments have no built-in refund or chargeback mechanism, unlike cards where issuers help dispute charges. — [Circle, "Refund Protocol: Non-custodial dispute resolution for stablecoin payments," Apr 17, 2025 (page modified May 4, 2026)](https://www.circle.com/blog/refund-protocol-non-custodial-dispute-resolution-for-stablecoin-payments)
- The x402 `auth-capture` spec contrasts itself with `exact`: `exact` "moves a fixed amount once and offers no way to give it back"; its comparison table lists `exact` as Reversible: "No". — [x402 spec, `specs/schemes/auth-capture/scheme_auth_capture.md`, v1.1 dated 2026-08-18](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/auth-capture/scheme_auth_capture.md)
- Commentary: on a per-call agent rail, a refund is just a new transfer to the paying wallet, which may be ephemeral; advice is to fix the refund destination before the first purchase (single opinion piece). — [Stablecoin Insider, "Refunds when an agent initiated the payment" (undated in results)](https://stablecoininsider.org/refunds-when-an-agent-initiated-the-payment/)

**Escrow / auth-capture contracts (LIVE, production)**
- Coinbase/Base **Commerce Payments Protocol**: "A permissionless protocol for onchain payments that mimics traditional 'authorize and capture' payment flows"; operations authorize, capture, charge, void, reclaim, refund; void is an operator action, reclaim is payer-initiated "Recover expired authorizations". README has no dispute or chargeback language and does not say refunds are mandatory. Audits: Coinbase Protocol Security (Mar 19, Mar 26, Apr 15, 2025) and Spearbit (Apr 1, Apr 22, 2025; v1.1.0 audit Jul 22, 2026). — [base/commerce-payments README](https://github.com/base/commerce-payments)
- Shopify engineering: reclaim is a payer-initiated void available only after authorization expires; payment transitions only occur within the payment's expiry windows; partial capture supported. Coinbase Commerce rebuilt checkout on the protocol, deployed on Base (USDC). — [Shopify Engineering, "Commerce payments protocol" (2025)](https://shopify.engineering/commerce-payments-protocol); [Fintech Wrap Up deep dive](https://www.fintechwrapup.com/p/deep-dive-coinbases-commerce-payments)
- **x402 `auth-capture` scheme v1.1 (2026-08-18; SPECIFIED, EVM binding on the audited `AuthCaptureEscrow`)**: lifecycle authorize/charge/capture/void/refund/reclaim; `reclaim` is "the client's unilateral escape hatch" (only for an untouched hold after the capture deadline); `refundDeadline` is "the last moment a refund can be issued"; and critically "`charge` (the settled amount), `capture`, `void`, and `refund` are not client-authorized" and require resource-server (merchant) consent. Example use case listed: "Refundable payments with buyer protection." The EVM binding names a "Refund funding" open item ("who supplies refund liquidity"). — [x402 auth-capture scheme](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/auth-capture/scheme_auth_capture.md); [EVM binding](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/auth-capture/scheme_auth_capture_evm.md)
- x402 governance reportedly moved to the Linux Foundation in April 2026 (secondary; not verified against an LF announcement). — [search digest citing SoftwareSeni, "x402 and Machine Payments Protocol"](https://www.softwareseni.com/x402-machine-payments-protocol-and-the-card-versus-stablecoin-debate/)

**Arbiter-based escrow (RESEARCH / PROPOSAL / HACKATHON)**
- **Circle Refund Protocol** (Apr 17, 2025): payer calls `pay` into escrow recording recipient, amount and a payer-chosen refund address; arbiter sets a lockup; recipient can `refundByRecipient`; if recipient refuses, payer appeals and arbiter can `refundByArbiter`; arbiter can allow early withdrawal for a fee via recipient-signed EIP-712 terms; arbiter can front a refund and record a debt owed by the recipient. Arbiter **cannot** send escrowed funds to an address of its choosing (non-custodial). Caveats stated by Circle: a malicious arbiter could pay from another address and approve unwarranted refunds. **Status: research project, "not been audited," "educational purposes only," Apache-2.0**, code at github.com/circlefin/refund-protocol. The article does not mention AI agents. — [Circle blog, Apr 17, 2025](https://www.circle.com/blog/refund-protocol-non-custodial-dispute-resolution-for-stablecoin-payments); [CryptoSlate, Apr 2025](https://cryptoslate.com/circle-introduces-refund-protocol-to-enable-dispute-resolution-in-stablecoin-payments/)
- **x402r** (refund extension for x402): v2 holds payments in escrow and an independent arbiter resolves disputes; pitched for agents and humans. Early-stage/product-launch listing. — [x402r on Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/x402r-refund-protocol)
- **Hackathon projects (ETHGlobal Buenos Aires)**: "x402 R&D" (merchant refunds, user disputes, neutral agent adjudicators, AI-assisted low-value decisions) and "OkayOkayOkay" (escrow locked "as long as the legal framework allows for chargebacks"). — [ETHGlobal x402r and D](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/x402r-and-d-1k9yg); [ETHGlobal okayokayokay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/okayokayokay-043ic)
- **Kleros** (decentralized arbitration on Ethereum): jurors drawn from PNK stakers, majority-coherent jurors rewarded and incoherent lose stake; appeals double the jury plus one; smart contracts can escrow disputed funds pending ruling. Sources are mostly low-quality secondary; a Stanford socio-legal study flags procedural-safeguard concerns with anonymous, economically incentivized jurors. — [Stanford Law, "Kleros: A Socio-Legal Case Study of Decentralized Justice & Blockchain Arbitration"](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/kleros-a-socio-legal-case-study-of-decentralized-justice-blockchain-arbitration/); [Kluwer Arbitration Blog](https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/?p=46151)

**Issuer freeze powers (LIVE, but law-enforcement/court channel)**
- GENIUS Act (Pub. L. 119-27, Jul 18, 2025) defines "lawful order" as a final and valid order under Federal law, from a court of competent jurisdiction or authorized Federal agency, that "(A) requires a person to seize, freeze, burn, or prevent the transfer of payment stablecoins issued by the person; (B) specifies the payment stablecoins or accounts subject to blocking with reasonable particularity; and (C) is subject to judicial or administrative review or appeal." Issuers (and, for US distribution, foreign issuers) must have "the technological capability to comply, and will comply, with the terms of any lawful order." — [Public Law 119-27 text, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)
- Penalties: up to $1M per violation per day for non-compliant foreign issuers offering in the US; $100K/day for digital asset service providers trading them. — [Covington, "The GENIUS Act Becomes Law," Jul 25, 2025](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/the-genius-act-becomes-law-key-provisions-from-the-federal-stablecoin-regulatory-framework)
- Circle/CENTRE (2022 statement): complies with binding court orders with appropriate jurisdiction; a blacklisted address cannot receive USDC and its USDC cannot be transferred on-chain. — [Seizure of Frozen Crypto Wallets on Circle (law-firm page quoting CENTRE)](https://criminaldefenseattorneytampa.com/asset-seizure-asset-forfeiture/cryptocurrency/circle/)
- Court-driven freezes in private civil cases: ~$57.65M USDC frozen after an SDNY TRO in the LIBRA case (Burwick Law) — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/regulation/circle-freezes-57-million-usdc-linked-to-libra-team-following-burwick-law-court-e40169ad); a TRO-driven freeze on Zama's shared cUSDC wrapper (locked ~$12.6M of other users' funds) was lifted by an N.D. Cal. judge after ~3 days, described as the first court-ordered Circle contract-level blacklist in a private civil dispute unwound through litigation (date not captured) — [The Defiant, lift](https://thedefiant.io/us-court-lifts-circle-freeze-zama-cusdc-three-day-lockout); [The Defiant, freeze](https://thedefiant.io/circle-freeze-on-zamas-confidential-usdc-locks-12-6m-of-user-funds-in-defi-crossfire)
- Scale: Tether said in Feb 2026 it had frozen ~$4.2B USDT linked to illegal activity (~$3.5B since 2023); BlockSec counted 4,163 addresses / $1.26B USDT frozen in 2025, with >50% (~$698M) later destroyed via `destroyBlackFunds`; AMLBot: Circle froze $109M across 372 addresses in 2023–2025 (USDT freezes ~30x USDC). A BlockSec "$514M in 30 days" figure has an unclear date window. — [Cointelegraph (BlockSec)](https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-freezes-over-500m-usdt-30-days-blocksec-data-show); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/tether-froze-514m-usdt-30-days); [Whale Alert (Elliptic/AMLBot figures)](https://whale-alert.io/stories/c690013e01d9b5/Tether-froze-42B-in-USDT-over-3-years-including-544M-at-Turkeys-request-EllipticCircle-blacklisted-5700-wallets-25B)
- Claimed mechanics (low-quality secondary, unverified): Tether burns frozen balances, Circle blocks without burning, Paxos's contract behind PYUSD can wipe a frozen address. — [Coincub, "Can Stablecoin Issuers Freeze Funds Without a Court Order?"](https://coincub.com/blog/stablecoin-issuers-freeze-funds/)

### Inferences
- After capture, every live stablecoin rail found (Commerce Payments Protocol, x402 `auth-capture`) makes refunds **merchant-consented**: the payer's only unilateral right is `reclaim` of an uncaptured, expired hold. "My AI bought that" after capture is therefore a request to the merchant, not a right against the rail.
- The only designs with a neutral decision-maker (Circle Refund Protocol, x402r, Kleros, ADRP) are research/proposal/hackathon-stage as of Oct 2026.
- Freezes require a particular, reviewable Federal order (GENIUS) or issuer policy for illicit activity; none of the issuers' practices found offer freezing as a consumer "unauthorized purchase" remedy, and freezing does not return funds to the payer by itself.

### Gaps
- Circle's current published USDC blacklisting policy text and Paxos/PYUSD freeze/wipe policy were not retrieved from primary pages.
- Whether Circle Refund Protocol has been audited, productized, or used by any agent platform since Apr 2025: not found.
- No 2026 USDC freeze totals found; no data on any freeze used to reverse an agent purchase.
- Kleros' current escrow product and any agent-commerce use: no primary source read.

## 2. Emerging dispute standards for agent payments (AP2, x402, Visa/Mastercard rules, reason codes)

### Takeaway
No network or protocol has an enacted, agent-specific dispute rule or reason code as of Oct 2026. AP2 v0.2 defines what evidence a dispute should use (mandates + receipts) and how to verify it, but explicitly leaves "dispute resolution, retention, and retrieval" out of scope and imagines a card network as adjudicator; for stablecoins it only points to an a2a-x402 sample. Mastercard's Verifiable Intent and Visa's CE 3.0 changes are evidence formats, not liability rules.

### Cited Findings
**AP2 (Google-led; spec v0.2; LIVE spec, dispute process OUT OF SCOPE)**
- Spec intro lists "How the Checkout and Payment Mandates can be used as evidence at the time of dispute." Section "Dispute Evidence": the Checkout Mandate and Receipt and Payment Mandate and Receipt "can be brought together to provide a non-repudiable picture of the transaction. Specific details of how this is used for dispute resolution, retention, and retrieval requirements are outside the scope of this specification." Checkout Mandate/Receipt may come from the Shopping Agent or Merchant; Payment Mandate/Receipt from Shopping Agent, Credential Provider, Network, or Merchant Payment Processor. — [AP2 `docs/ap2/specification.md` (v0.2, main @ Apr 29, 2026)](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- "Verification: Dispute" MUST steps: verify the Checkout Mandate per merchant rules; independently recompute the `checkout_jwt` hash; Checkout Receipt `reference` must equal the hash of the closed Checkout Mandate; verify the Payment Mandate against `checkout_hash`; Payment Receipt reference must match the closed Payment Mandate hash. Only then can the mandates be used "as evidence as to what the user, and each role saw." — [same spec](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- A note says automated retrieval of the Checkout Mandate (keyed by the Payment Mandate `transaction_id`) "would provide substantial utility" but is outside the current version. Implementation guidance: for dispute resolution, store the SD-JWTs with disclosures in compact serialization. — [spec](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [implementation_considerations.md](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/implementation_considerations.md)
- FAQ: "In a dispute, the network adjudicator (e.g., Card Network) can use the user-signed Checkout Mandate and compare the details of what was agreed upon ... against the details in the dispute." On crypto: AP2 is payment-agnostic; "As a first step" see google-agentic-commerce/a2a-x402, "We will be aligning this closely with AP2 over time ... including stablecoins." — [AP2 FAQ](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/faq.md)
- PayPal (Sep 16, 2025) listed as an AP2 opportunity: extend Seller Protection workflows with AP2 mandates, mandate IDs and signatures as dispute-grade evidence. *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [PayPal Developer blog, "PayPal and the Agent Payments Protocol," Sep 16, 2025](https://developer.paypal.com/community/blog/PayPal-Agent-Payments-Protocol/)

**AP2 dispute add-ons (PROPOSAL, individual IETF drafts)**
- **ADRP (Agent Dispute Resolution Protocol)**, B. Stone (SwarmSync.AI): adds a fourth "Arbitration Mandate" pre-signed by the principal at deployment (arbitration pool, governing law, fees, escalation thresholds); splits disputes into a cryptographic class (decidable from proof bundle and mandate chain) and a semantic class (decided against pre-committed acceptance criteria, else arbitration); append-only dispute event chain; outputs an escrow directive (release/refund/split). Individual draft -00 (Apr 2026), -01 (Sep 2026); not adopted. — [draft-stone-adrp-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-stone-adrp-00); [draft-stone-adrp-01 (Sep 2026)](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-stone-adrp-01.html)
- VCAP–AP2 binding draft: on dispute, implementations SHOULD package the AP2 binding, service agreement, delivery evidence and verifier evidence for ADRP or another agreed mechanism. — [draft-stone-vcap-ap2-binding-01](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-stone-vcap-ap2-binding-01.html)

**x402 (LIVE protocol; no dispute mechanism in core)**
- The core spec v2 defines `upfront` and `escrow` settlement flows; the only refund mechanics are in the `auth-capture` and `upto` schemes (merchant/server-driven refunds; `upto` refunds unsettled remainder; server "MUST NOT charge for work it did not deliver" and settles a refund after a `>= 400` failure). No dispute, arbiter, or chargeback concept in the specs directory. — [x402 spec v2](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md); [upto SVM scheme](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/upto/scheme_upto_svm.md)

**Card networks (evidence formats, no agent liability rule)**
- Worldpay (Jul 30, 2026): no liability shift yet exists for agent transactions. *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [Worldpay, "Agentic commerce liability is still being written"](https://www.worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-liability-is-still-being-written)
- Mastercard **Verifiable Intent** (Mar 5, 2026): open standard for a tamper-resistant record of what a user authorized, pitched for dispute resolution; contributed to FIDO Alliance alongside AP2 (Apr 28, 2026); Agent Pay integration "in the coming months." *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [Mastercard, Mar 5, 2026](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/stories/2026/verifiable-intent.html); [FIDO Alliance, Apr 28, 2026](https://fidoalliance.org/fido-alliance-to-develop-standards-for-trusted-ai-agent-interactions/)
- Visa **Compelling Evidence 3.0** (reason code 10.4): merchant can win with two prior undisputed transactions 120–365 days old matching on ≥2 identity elements (one being IP or device ID); April 18, 2026 Visa Core Rules extend CE 3.0 to multi-merchant transactions from Oct 24, 2026; secondary sources say the edition recognizes login IDs for agentic payment providers (rule text not read). *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [Visa CE 3.0 merchant readiness, Mar 2023](https://usa.review.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/regional/na/us/support-legal/documents/compelling-evidence-3.0-merchant-readiness-mar2023.pdf); [Visa Core Rules](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/download/about-visa/visa-rules-public.pdf); [Pulse, Sep 24, 2026](https://pulsecrm.com/blog/visa-compelling-evidence-3-multi-merchant-change-october-2026/)
- American Express announced an Agent Purchase Protection for cardmembers (Apr 14, 2026). *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [Digital Commerce 360, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/04/14/american-express-agentic-commerce-developer-kit-purchase-protection/)
- No agent-specific reason code at Visa or Mastercard and no standard field for mandate content in dispute files; card network rules show no distinction between human- and AI-initiated disputes (vendor commentary; Chargeflow sells chargeback services). — [Chargeflow, "AI agent chargeback liability"](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/ai-agent-chargeback-liability); [Chargeflow, "Agentic commerce regulation"](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/agentic-commerce-regulation-what-merchants-need-to-know); [Checkout.com, "Chargebacks in agentic commerce"](https://www.checkout.com/blog/chargebacks-in-agentic-commerce-how-merchants-can-stay-ahead)
- Single-vendor forecast (not a Mastercard statement): first binding network rule on agent disputes no earlier than the April 2027 release cycle; Oct 2026 releases focus on identity/token/authentication; cites a Mastercard "Signals" report (Aug 3, 2026) treating liability and dispute resolution as unsolved. — [Shopappy, "Agent chargeback rules, holiday 2026"](https://shopappy.com/payments/card-networks/agent-chargeback-rules-holiday-2026)
- Chargebacks911's CEO describes a new dispute category where agents execute purchases exactly as instructed but consumers claim they did not want them. — [Chargeflow (citing Chargebacks911)](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/ai-agent-chargeback-liability)

### Inferences
- Card disputes for agent purchases run under existing reason codes; the new artifacts (Verifiable Intent, AP2 mandates, CE 3.0 login IDs) only strengthen evidence. None of them reaches a pure stablecoin push payment, because there is no issuer/network dispute process to submit evidence into.
- AP2's own FAQ assumes a "network adjudicator (e.g., Card Network)"; for x402/stablecoins, AP2 currently names no adjudicator at all.

### Gaps
- Primary Visa/Mastercard rule text on agent-initiated transactions not read this session (Visa Core Rules PDF and Mastercard Chargeback Guide not fetched).
- Visa Trusted Agent Protocol's dispute role: only secondary descriptions found.
- a2a-x402 repo not inspected for any refund/dispute handling.
- Whether any card program treats a card-funded stablecoin purchase (e.g., buying USDC with a card, then an agent spends it) as disputable beyond the on-ramp purchase: not researched.

## 3. Regulation: Reg E / EFTA, GENIUS Act, EU MiCA/PSD2, CFPB positions, 2026 agent-payment rules

### Takeaway
US: the GENIUS Act (enacted Jul 18, 2025) contains no unauthorized-transaction or error-resolution regime (the text never mentions EFTA, Regulation E, "unauthorized," or "error resolution"); it preserves state consumer-protection law, gives custody customers priority, and requires disclosed redemption policies. The CFPB's Jan 2025 proposal to bring stablecoin/crypto transfers under EFTA was withdrawn May 15, 2025, and no 2026 CFPB action on stablecoins or agent payments was found. EU: from Mar 2, 2026, CASPs transferring e-money tokens (EMTs) that qualify as payment services must comply fully with PSD2 — which by inference brings PSD2's unauthorised-transaction rules, but no source confirms that application.

### Cited Findings
**GENIUS Act (ENACTED, Pub. L. 119-27)**
- Signed Jul 18, 2025; effective on the earlier of 18 months after enactment (i.e., Jan 18, 2027) or 120 days after the primary Federal payment stablecoin regulators issue final implementing regulations (Sec. 20). — [Public Law 119-27, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)
- Text search of the enacted law: zero occurrences of "Electronic Fund", "Regulation E", "unauthorized", or "error resolution". — [Public Law 119-27](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)
- Consumer-protection hooks that do exist: issuers must "publicly disclose the issuer's redemption policy" with "clear and conspicuous procedures for timely redemption" and plain-language fee disclosure; custody customers' claims to payment stablecoins have priority over other creditors' claims; "nothing in this Act shall preempt State consumer protection laws, including common law, and the remedies available thereunder." — [Public Law 119-27](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)
- Payment stablecoins from permitted issuers are excluded from "security" and "commodity" definitions. — [Covington, Jul 25, 2025](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/the-genius-act-becomes-law-key-provisions-from-the-federal-stablecoin-regulatory-framework)
- Advocacy view (Consumer Reports, Jul 2025): the Act "does not protect consumers if there are unauthorized or fraudulent transfers"; card users have federal protection, GENIUS provides none for unauthorized use; no required complaint/error process; no private right of action. — [Consumer Reports letter re S.1582, Jul 14, 2025](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/07-14-25-Consumer-Reports-Letter-Re-GENIUS-Act-S1582.pdf); [Consumer Reports press release on House passage](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/house-passes-genius-act-that-fails-to-protect-consumers-in-stablecoin-market)
- Law-firm view (Stinson): the Act treats issuers as financial institutions for the Bank Secrecy Act but is silent on whether they are "financial institutions" under EFTA/Reg E; that question may be left to states for now. — [Stinson, Witherspoon in Law360](https://www.stinson.com/newsroom-news-witherspoon-examines-stablecoin-regulation-in-law360)
- Implementation: OCC proposed comprehensive GENIUS rules (Feb/Mar 2026), treating the Act's consumer-protection provisions as self-executing and inviting comment on codifying them; proposal required timely par redemption, generally within two business days. — [Orrick, Feb 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/02/OCC-Proposes-Rule-to-Implement-the-GENIUS-Act); [Mayer Brown, Mar 11, 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/es/insights/publications/2026/03/occ-proposes-comprehensive-rulemaking-to-implement-the-genius-act). OCC reportedly finalized its rule (reported Oct 2026; a MarketsMedia item dated Oct 22, 2026 also reports it), covering reserves, redemption, risk management, custody and capital, with AML/sanctions left to a separate Treasury rulemaking. — [Decrypt, "OCC finalizes GENIUS Act stablecoin rules ahead of January deadline"](https://decrypt.co/381502/occ-finalizes-genius-act-stablecoin-rules-ahead-of-january-deadline); [MarketsMedia](https://www.marketsmedia.com/?p=159223)

**Reg E / EFTA and CFPB**
- Jan 10, 2025: CFPB proposed interpretive rule would have treated stablecoin/crypto transactions in connection with a consumer asset account as EFTs under Reg E (PROPOSAL). May 15, 2025: CFPB Federal Register notice withdrew it (WITHDRAWN). — [Innovative Payments Association, Mar 19, 2025 update](https://www.ipa.org/government-update/march-19-2025); [Cogent Law, "Stablecoin payments and the EFTA"](https://cogentlaw.com/stablecoin-payments-and-the-efta); [CCUA on CFPB withdrawals](https://www.ccua.org/cfpb-withdraws-three-proposed-rules-search-permanent-director-continues)
- Troutman (Jan 22, 2026): CFPB also withdrew several advisory opinions/interpretations; Congress repealed (via CRA) the CFPB rule defining larger participants for consumer digital payment apps. — [Troutman Pepper, "How payments law landscape will evolve in 2026," Jan 22, 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/how-payments-law-landscape-will-evolve-in-2026/)
- Cogent Law: the withdrawal means Reg E does not apply on a blanket basis for now, but nothing prevents a future administration from adopting a similar interpretation. — [Cogent Law](https://cogentlaw.com/stablecoin-payments-and-the-efta)
- Reg E's authorization rule as it applies where it does apply (e.g., fiat PayPal balance, debit): a transfer by a person the consumer gave access to is not "unauthorized" unless the consumer has notified the institution; a person exceeding authority leaves the consumer liable until notice. *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [12 CFR 1005.2(m)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/2/); [Official Interpretation 2(m)-2](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/interp-2/)
- Commentary: Reg E defines authorization as the consumer granting permission, leaving open whether an AI agent's action counts. — [Chargeflow, "Agentic commerce regulation"](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/agentic-commerce-regulation-what-merchants-need-to-know)

**EU: MiCA + PSD2 (ENACTED; transitional period ENDED Mar 2, 2026)**
- EBA No-Action Letter (Jun 2, 2025, EBA/Op/2025/08) gave CASPs nine months to keep transacting EMTs that qualify as payment services while seeking PSD2 authorisation. — [Mondaq, "MiCA EMTs: EBA advises industry as grace period is ending"](https://www.mondaq.com/fintech/1746044/mica-emts-eba-advises-industry-as-grace-period-is-ending); [EBA, ARTs and EMTs (MiCA)](https://eba.europa.eu/regulation-and-policy/asset-referenced-and-e-money-tokens-mica)
- EBA Opinion (Feb 12, 2026): after Mar 2, 2026, EMT transactions that qualify as payment services must comply fully with PSD2, otherwise cessation and customer offboarding; executing EMT transfers (and custody) may be a payment service whether or not wallets are treated as payment accounts (in scope: storage combined with transfers, internal/first-party transfers, pay-outs, certain custodial/omnibus wallet models). Morgan Lewis' note does not address PSD2 user protections (Art. 73 refunds, liability, SCA). — [Morgan Lewis, Feb 13, 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/02/eba-issues-supervisory-priorities-as-psd2-mica-transition-period-for-emt-activities-ends); [BDO Malta, "PSD2–MiCA interplay"](https://www.bdo.com.mt/en-gb/news/news-in-2026/psd2%E2%80%93mica-interplay-what-happens-after-2-march-2026)
- More than 100 entities had applied for the relevant authorisations, with many procedures ongoing (early 2026). — [Dudkowiak, "EBA on the end of the PSD2–MiCA transitional period"](https://www.dudkowiak.com/blog/eba-on-the-end-of-the-psd2-mica-transitional-period-what-will-change-after-2-march-2026/)

### Inferences
- US: a consumer whose agent spends USDC/PYUSD from a self-custodied wallet has no federal unauthorized-transfer right; from a custodial account, protection is whatever the custodian's contract says (see §4 for PayPal) plus any state law. GENIUS adds redemption and insolvency protection, not dispute rights.
- EU: if EMT transfers by a CASP are PSD2 payment services, PSD2's unauthorised-transaction refund and liability rules (Arts. 73–74 of Directive (EU) 2015/2366, not re-read this session — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj)) would plausibly attach to custodial EMT transfers. This is an inference; no source confirmed it, and self-custodied wallet transfers would remain outside PSD2.
- Even where Reg E/PSD2 apply, a delegated agent acting within (or beyond) granted authority is generally the consumer's responsibility until revocation — the same structure PayPal's crypto terms adopt (§4).

### Gaps
- No 2026 CFPB rule, RFI, or guidance on AI-agent-initiated payments was found; the Federal Register withdrawal notice itself was not retrieved.
- Treasury's GENIUS rulemakings and whether the OCC final rule triggers the Sec. 20 "120 days after final regulations" clock are unverified; the statutory outside date is Jan 18, 2027.
- No state law (e.g., NY DFS, California DFAL) unauthorized-transfer provisions for stablecoins were researched.
- MiCA's own text on holder rights (redemption at par for EMTs) and the PSD3/PSR status (including any agent-payment or APP-fraud refund provisions) were not researched this session.

## 4. How PayPal handles disputes for PYUSD / crypto (Purchase Protection, unauthorized transfers)

### Takeaway
PayPal's crypto terms (May 19, 2026) cover unauthorized crypto purchases/sales and reimburse unauthorized transfers out of the Crypto Account up to a $50,000 lifetime cap — but explicitly exclude cases where you "grant authority to someone" who "exceed[s] the authority you gave them," until you notify PayPal. On-chain transfers are irreversible. Purchase Protection is a separate program for purchases from sellers who accept PayPal; crypto as the item bought is, by inference, ineligible ("Financial products or investments of any kind"), Checkout with Crypto purchases are not on the exclusion list, and on-chain PYUSD sends to external wallets have no purchase-dispute path.

### Cited Findings
- **Unauthorized crypto activity coverage**: "PayPal Digital will protect you from the unauthorized purchase or sale of Crypto Assets in your Crypto Account, including for unauthorized sales used to Checkout with Crypto"; for unauthorized transfers it "will reimburse you in the form and quantity of the Crypto Asset that was removed from your account, up to a maximum lifetime cap of an equivalent of $50,000.00 U.S. dollars," provided reporting procedures are followed "and provided we determine that the transfer was unauthorized." Reimbursement "represents a warranty of our own services. PayPal Digital does not provide insurance." — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms, last updated May 19, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- **Definition**: an Unauthorized Transaction is crypto "purchased in, sold from, or transferred out of your Crypto Account, which you did not authorize and do not benefit you." It is NOT unauthorized if transfers go to an account from which you benefit; you act with fraudulent intent; "or you grant authority to someone to use your Crypto Account (by giving them your login information) and they exceed the authority you gave them. You are responsible for transactions made in this situation unless you have previously notified PayPal Digital that you no longer authorize transfers by that individual." — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms, May 19, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- **Claim conditions**: you may not sell/transfer the disputed assets while PayPal investigates; doing so (including via Checkout with Crypto) gets the claim denied; double recovery from PayPal and a bank/card issuer is a restricted activity. — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- **Irreversibility**: external crypto transfers are irreversible even while pending/unconfirmed. Disputes between the user and PayPal Digital go to individual arbitration. — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- **Checkout with Crypto mechanics**: PayPal Digital sells the crypto (no fee, but a spread) and the purchase settles as a PayPal payment; PayPal Balance statements include Checkout with Crypto purchases. — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc). At launch (Mar 30, 2021) PayPal said "eligible purchases made using Checkout with Crypto will also include the safety, security and other benefits of using PayPal." — [PayPal Newsroom, Mar 30, 2021](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2021-03-30-PayPal-Launches-Checkout-with-Crypto)
- **Purchase Protection scope**: "When you purchase something from a seller who accepts PayPal, you may be eligible for a refund under PayPal's Purchase Protection program"; PayPal decides eligibility "in its sole discretion." — [PayPal US User Agreement, last updated Sep 14, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/useragreement-full). Unauthorized-transaction claims are "different from the Purchase Protection program." Ineligible items include "Payments that are equivalent to cash, including stored value items such as gift cards and pre-paid cards," "Financial products or investments of any kind," and NFTs; crypto-funded purchases are not listed as ineligible. — [PayPal Buyer Protection terms, last updated Jan 26, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/buyer-protection)
- **Definitions**: in the User Agreement "funds" means sovereign-currency money "and not cryptocurrency"; cryptocurrency services are provided by PayPal Digital, Inc. — [PayPal US User Agreement, Sep 14, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/useragreement-full)
- **PYUSD transfers**: PYUSD can be sent to external wallets that support it (network fees apply); limits differ across PayPal help-page versions ($10K vs $25K/week send). Help page repeats the $50,000 lifetime unauthorized-transfer cap and "not insurance." — [PayPal Help, "What is PayPal USD (PYUSD)?"](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-is-paypal-usd-pyusd-HELP1005)
- PayPal "Agent Ready" says Seller and Purchase Protection, fraud tools and chargeback coverage extend to agentic transactions; PayPal's Apr 30, 2026 Seller Protection article. *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched; neither addresses crypto)* — [PayPal AI Agent Ready](https://www.paypal.ai/us/agent-ready); [PayPal, "How PayPal's Seller Protection is evolving for the agentic commerce era," Apr 30, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/seller-protection-agentic-commerce)

### Inferences
- Three distinct PayPal cases: (a) crypto bought as the item (from PayPal Digital or a seller) → no Purchase Protection (financial product); (b) goods bought with Checkout with Crypto → settles as a PayPal payment, not excluded by the Jan 2026 list, so plausibly eligible, but no 2026 PayPal document confirms it; (c) PYUSD sent on-chain to an external wallet/merchant (e.g., via x402) → a transfer, not a PayPal purchase, so no Purchase Protection and no merchant dispute; only the $50K unauthorized-transfer warranty, and only if the user never authorized the sender.
- "My AI bought that" for a crypto spend by an agent the user set up is, under PayPal's own crypto terms, the "exceeded the authority you gave them" case — consumer-liable until revocation — mirroring Reg E §1005.2(m) and PayPal's fiat User Agreement. The deciding fact is the scope of what was delegated and when it was revoked, which is exactly the record Readback produces on the fiat/vault rail.

### Gaps
- No PayPal document found (2025–2026) that states Purchase Protection eligibility for Checkout with Crypto or PYUSD-funded purchases in current terms; the crypto terms grep found no "Purchase Protection" text.
- Whether PayPal's merchant-side "Pay with Crypto" (crypto received by merchants, converted to USD) carries buyer disputes: not researched.
- Whether an agent holding a delegated API credential (rather than "login information") is treated the same as the T&C's login-sharing example: not addressed in the terms.

## 5. Evidence: what record a disputing party would use in crypto, and who adjudicates

### Takeaway
The available evidence is strong on *what was signed* and weak on *what the human intended*: an on-chain transaction hash plus the payer's signed transfer authorization (ERC-3009/Permit2/EIP-712), and, where used, AP2 mandates and receipts. By default no one adjudicates; in practice the ladder is merchant → a pre-named contract arbiter (where escrow was used) → card network (only if card-funded) → PayPal (only if PayPal-custodied) → courts (which can obtain an issuer freeze).

### Cited Findings
- x402/auth-capture EVM: the client produces exactly one signature — an ERC-3009 or Permit2 authorization naming a collector — and the escrow gates operations on `msg.sender == paymentInfo.operator`; the operator is committed on-chain as `PaymentInfo.operator`. — [x402 auth-capture EVM binding](https://github.com/coinbase/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/auth-capture/scheme_auth_capture_evm.md)
- Circle Refund Protocol records recipient, amount, refund address on-chain at payment; early-withdrawal terms are recipient-signed EIP-712 messages verified on-chain; the arbiter is the adjudicator, constrained to refund only to the pre-set address. — [Circle blog, Apr 17, 2025](https://www.circle.com/blog/refund-protocol-non-custodial-dispute-resolution-for-stablecoin-payments)
- AP2 evidence bundle = Checkout Mandate + Receipt + Payment Mandate + Receipt, verified by recomputed hashes; adjudicator envisioned as "the network adjudicator (e.g., Card Network)"; retention/retrieval out of scope. — [AP2 spec v0.2](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [AP2 FAQ](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/faq.md)
- ADRP argues a valid cryptographic proof does not by itself show the contract was satisfied; it separates crypto-checkable from semantic disputes and routes the latter to pre-agreed arbitration (PROPOSAL). — [draft-stone-adrp-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-stone-adrp-00)
- Card-side evidence gap: agent checkouts strip IP/device/browsing signals merchants normally use; Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol is described as capturing agent identity and signed order intent (secondary, still being formalized). — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/blog/chargebacks-in-agentic-commerce-how-merchants-can-stay-ahead); [Coincub, "AI Agent Chargebacks"](https://coincub.com/blog/ai-agent-chargebacks-liability-risks-for-merchants/)
- Kleros-style adjudication: staked, randomly drawn jurors vote on submitted evidence; escalating appeals (secondary). — [Stanford Law case study](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/kleros-a-socio-legal-case-study-of-decentralized-justice-blockchain-arbitration/)
- Courts can compel issuer freezes under GENIUS "lawful order" (particularity + reviewability required); private civil TROs have produced Circle freezes, one reversed in ~3 days. — [Public Law 119-27](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/us-court-lifts-circle-freeze-zama-cusdc-three-day-lockout)
- PayPal crypto disputes with PayPal Digital itself are resolved by PayPal investigation then individual arbitration. — [PayPal Cryptocurrency Terms, May 19, 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/paypal/cryptocurrencies-tnc)
- Large US enterprises on who should be liable for agent disputes: AI platform 46%, customer who authorized the agent 34%, merchant 11%, shared 5%, payment provider 3% (189 large enterprises of 498 merchants surveyed, fieldwork Feb–Mar 2026; card/PayPal context, not crypto-specific). *(repo-sourced, not re-fetched)* — [PayPal US Agentic Commerce Pulse, large enterprise report, Mar 2026](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/web26/us/agentic-commerce-pulse-report/paypal-agentic-commerce-report_le-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- An on-chain signature proves the wallet key authorized *that transfer*, not that the human authorized *that purchase*. Without a separately signed, user-approved scope (an AP2 intent/checkout mandate, Verifiable Intent, or a PayPal-approved agreement), a crypto dispute over agent intent has nothing to compare the transfer against.
- In crypto the evidence must be retained by the parties (AP2 says retention is out of scope; chains store only the transfer). This contrasts with a PayPal-vault design where the approved terms and signed mandate live in PayPal's own objects.
- Because refunds after capture are merchant-consented on every live rail, a merchant-side verifier that recommends refunds for out-of-scope or unsigned charges is the only "adjudicator" most stablecoin agent payments will see in 2026.

### Gaps
- No real-world adjudicated case of an agent-initiated stablecoin purchase dispute (merchant, arbiter, or court) was found.
- No standard evidence package format for stablecoin disputes exists; ADRP/VCAP are individual drafts.
